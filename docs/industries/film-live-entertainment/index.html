IN PLAIN ENGLISHEntertainment PR is getting a show, a film, or a live event talked about and written about in the places it plays, so the people nearby know it is coming, want to go, and buy the ticket before it leaves.

The Problems We Solve

WHERE ENTERTAINMENT PROMOTION FALLS FLAT

Arriving in a market cold

A national campaign does not make a local newsroom care. We build the local angle for each market: the hometown performer, the community tie-in, the story that gives an editor a reason to cover it.

Press timed for the wrong week

Coverage that lands after opening night sells nothing. We plan press backward from the run: advance features, opening-week coverage, and the reminders that move the final performances.

Buzz that never becomes tickets

Attention is not the goal; attendance is. Every piece of promotion we run points at the box office, with tracking on links and offers, so we know what sold seats.

Local partners left on the table

Schools, community groups, local businesses, and media partners can put a show in front of thousands of the right people. We line them up before the trucks arrive.

Social that looks like a press release

Feeds stop for real moments: the load-in, the rehearsal, the face in the crowd. We build those into ads in the production's voice and put paid reach behind what earns attention in that market.

No plan for the day the run ends

The coverage, the photos, and the partner relationships should be working for the next tour and the next city. We make sure they are captured and reusable.

Questions, Answered

WHAT PRODUCERS AND PROMOTERS ASK US

What does PR for a touring show or live event involve?

PR for a touring show means making each market care before it arrives: local press angles, advance and opening-week coverage, community and media partners, and social ads that show real moments. All of it is pointed at ticket sales during the run, with tracking so we know what worked.

How far ahead should promotion start for a run?

Promotion should start further ahead than feels natural. We plan backward from the dates: partners and advance press first, then the buildup, then reminders that move the final performances. Announcing the week of is how closing night plays to empty seats.

Do you handle film as well as live events?

Yes, we have promoted independent film alongside touring productions and live events. The mechanics differ, but the job is the same: find the local angle, earn the coverage, and point the attention at the screening or the seat.

Do you require long-term contracts?

No, monitoring and marketing plans run month to month, and you can walk anytime. Anything we built or set up for you is yours to keep. We keep clients by delivering results, not by locking the door.
