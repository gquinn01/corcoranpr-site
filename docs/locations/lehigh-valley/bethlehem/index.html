What a Site in Bethlehem Has to Get Right

SIX WAYS THIS CITY SHAPES A WEBSITE

Atmosphere

Atmosphere that replaces the answer

A header photograph of the street is not a service page. Set the scene in the top third if you like, but the visitor came with a question and will leave if the first screen does not begin to answer it.

Hospitality

Restaurants and hospitality downtown

A lively downtown means a visitor is choosing between several good options tonight. Current hours, tonight's menu or list, how to book, and where to park. The photographs sell the room; the details decide the booking.

Professional

Professional firms in a city with charm

A charming address does not qualify a firm. Clients here still want to know who does the work, what engagement looks like, and what you handle best. Say that first, and let the city be the backdrop rather than the argument.

Industry

Industrial and trade businesses

Industry here sells to buyers who do not care how pretty the town is. Capability, capacity, hours, and coverage, stated plainly and reachable in one click, are worth more than any amount of local flavor.

Stock

Stock imagery in a distinctive place

Generic photographs stand out badly in a city people recognize. Real pictures of your premises, your work, and your people cost an afternoon and outperform a stock library every time, especially where visitors have already seen the real thing.

Visitors

Being found by people who are not from here

A good share of your customers are visiting or new to the area. They search plain words, not local shorthand, so the site has to name what you do in the terms a stranger would use, alongside whatever you are known as locally.

Questions, Answered

WHAT BETHLEHEM BUSINESS OWNERS ASK US

How much of the local character should the website carry?

Enough to feel like you, never in place of the information. A good rule is that a stranger should know what you do and how to get it before they meet any atmosphere. Character earns the visit; clarity earns the booking.

A lot of our customers are visitors rather than locals. Does that change the site?

It changes the words. Visitors search in plain terms and do not know local shorthand, street names, or what a place is locally known for. The page needs both: the plain description a stranger would type, and the name locals use.

Do you have an office in Bethlehem?

No. We work out of Quakertown, a short drive south, and it is the only office we have. Most of what we do happens by phone, video, and email, and we come up when it helps.

Do you require long-term contracts?

No. Monitoring and marketing plans run month to month, and you can walk anytime. Anything we built for you, including your website, is yours to keep. We keep clients by delivering results, not by locking the door.
